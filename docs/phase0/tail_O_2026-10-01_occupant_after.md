```
Mold is a biological pollutant that can contribute to indoor air quality problems in several ways:

- **Allergen source** – Mold spores are tiny particles that can be inhaled, triggering allergic reactions or asthma in sensitive people.
- **Toxin production** – Some molds produce mycotoxins, which can irritate the skin, eyes, and respiratory tract.
- **Odor** – Growing mold often releases a musty smell, indicating moisture problems and potential health risks.
- **Surface damage** – Mold growth can degrade building materials, leading to structural issues and further indoor contamination.

The building’s records show that mold risk is monitored by tracking relative humidity. When humidity rises above the set thresholds (70 % RH for all floors), a mold‑risk alert is triggered. This helps prevent the conditions that allow mold to grow and thus protects indoor air quality.
```
