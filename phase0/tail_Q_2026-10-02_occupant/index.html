    **How to keep walkways clear in this building**

The building already measures a number of things that can help you spot and prevent walk‑way blockages without adding new sensors.  Below are concrete actions you can take now, with a short explanation of why each one works.

| # | Recommendation | Why it works (link to existing sensor) | Practical tip |
|---|----------------|----------------------------------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Use the Waste fill‑level sensor to schedule bin removal** | The waste fill‑level sensor reports the percentage of a bin’s capacity.  When the reading climbs to 80 % or higher, the bin is almost full and may spill or be moved into a corridor, blocking the walk‑way. | Set an alert (or a simple spreadsheet log) that triggers a cleaning crew to empty the bin when the level reaches 80 %.  This keeps bins from overflowing and from being placed in hallways. |
| 2 | **Monitor motion sensor counts for sudden drops** | Motion sensors give a count of detected movements in a zone.  A normal walk‑way will show a steady stream of motion during business hours.  If the count drops to zero or near‑zero for more than 5 minutes, something is likely blocking the path. | Create a rule: “If motion count < 5 for > 5 min, send a notification to the facilities team.”  This lets you react quickly before people get stuck. |
| 3 | **Check occupancy status during cleaning or maintenance windows** | Occupancy status tells you whether people are present in a space.  Walk‑ways are safest when no one is using the area. | Schedule routine cleaning or equipment relocation during periods when occupancy status is “low” or “none.”  If occupancy is high, postpone the activity or use a temporary sign to keep people out. |
| 4 | **Use door contact sensors to detect closed doors that block paths** | Door contact sensors report whether a door is open or closed.  A closed door in a corridor can block a walk‑way, especially if it’s a service door that should stay open. | Review the door contact logs for any doors that remain closed during peak hours.  If a door is closed for more than 10 minutes when it should be open, flag it for maintenance or change the door’s latch to keep it open. |
| 5 | **Track illuminance levels for signs of obstruction** | Illuminance sensors measure light intensity.  A sudden drop in light in a corridor can indicate that a large object (e.g., a stack of boxes or a bin) is blocking the path. | Set a threshold (e.g., 200 lux).  If illuminance falls below this level for more than 2 minutes, investigate the area for possible obstructions. |

### Priority summary
**Most important action first:**  
1. **Set up alerts for waste fill‑level > 80 %** – prevents bins from spilling into hallways.  
2. **Create a motion‑drop rule** – gives instant notice of a blocked path.  
3. **Schedule cleaning during low occupancy** – keeps walk‑ways clear when people are not around.  

Implementing these steps uses the sensors you already have and gives you a clear, data‑driven way to keep walkways free of obstructions.
